## Audio analysis

The uploaded audio was found in the connected GitHub repository as `merged-audio.mp3` and analysed as the source of truth:

- Duration: **427.99 seconds** (7:07.99)
- Audio: **mono, 48 kHz, approximately 135 kb/s MP3**
- The first sample uses 0:00–0:31.5 and includes the original narration in the MP4.
- The WebVTT has useful time ranges but contains substantial automatic-transcription errors. Captions in the sample are therefore cleaned paraphrases, while movement and cuts are anchored to the VTT ranges and the measured speech envelope.

The audio has frequent short pauses, so the animation uses pauses for reaction holds, prop hits and transitions rather than cutting on every word.

## Full-video visual chapters

These are the production chapters for the 7:08 narration. Each chapter keeps the same character rig, ink treatment and caption system while changing the real photographic plates and props.

1. **0:00–0:35 — The problem:** no device, no number and no reliable way to name the hour. Bedroom, sunrise and comic guessing gag.
2. **0:35–1:24 — Moon and sun:** lunar phases become a calendar; a stick and moving shadow introduce the sundial. Use moon-cycle time-lapse and a real desert/temple plate.
3. **1:24–2:34 — Water clocks:** Egyptian water-clock reconstruction, animated water level, engraved marks and a character trying to stop the leak with a finger.
4. **2:34–3:20 — Why sixty?:** Mesopotamian/Babylonian base-60 idea. Animate 60 splitting into 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12 and 30, with the character overwhelmed by arrows.
5. **3:20–4:18 — Measuring without a constant sky:** sundial, water, sand and candle clocks appear as real plates with illustrated overlays; each fails when its dependency disappears.
6. **4:18–5:08 — Mechanical clocks and the pendulum:** gears, weights and escapement first; then Huygens’ pendulum. The character rides the pendulum like a swing for the comic release.
7. **5:08–6:25 — Precision and standard time:** Harrison, longitude, railways and local solar times. Use a moving ship/map and a fast city-to-city time wipe.
8. **6:25–7:08 — Quartz, atomic clocks and CTA:** electronic/quartz/atomic progression, a vibration graphic, then the final “how long is a second?” payoff and subscribe beat.

## Style guardrails

- The character remains the same bald/short-haired, heavy-outline, sleepy-eyed puppet throughout; only pose, scale and expression change.
- Real photography is used as a scene plate, not as a static slideshow. Every plate receives drift/parallax, a light change, an animated prop or a camera move.
- Transitions use paper-card wipes, red ink marks, whip pans and occasional freeze frames. Avoid constant movement under dense narration; holds on reaction beats make the jokes readable.
- Captions stay short and high-contrast. The spoken audio remains authoritative; captions are not a word-for-word replacement for the noisy ASR text.
